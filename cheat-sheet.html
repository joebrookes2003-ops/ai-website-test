The one rule

Talk to it. Don't search it. More words is better, not worse.

For any awkward message — the four-part recipe

  1. Who you are and what happened
  2. What you want to happen
  3. The tone — firm / friendly / formal / apologetic
  4. How long — short / one paragraph / a page

Never send the first version. Say what to change, in plain words:

  • "Too formal — make it sound like a normal person"
  • "Shorter. Half that."
  • "Take out the last paragraph"
  • "Add that I've been a customer for eleven years"

The three safety rules

  1. Postcard rule — no bank details, passwords, NI number, passport number
  2. Check what matters — anything medical, legal, money, or with a date or number in it
  3. It agrees too easily — for a real opinion ask: "Give me the strongest argument against this"

Five things to try this week

  • Take a letter you don't understand: "What is this actually asking me to do?"
  • "Explain [anything] to me like I'm completely new to it"
  • "Here's what's in my fridge. Two of us, half an hour, I'm not a confident cook."
  • Write the message you've been putting off
  • "I'm going to [place] for four days in [month]. We like [x], we don't like [y]. What should we do?"

When it goes wrong

Tell it what's wrong and ask again. That's it. You can't break it, and it doesn't mind.
