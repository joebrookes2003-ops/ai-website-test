Refunds

If it's not for you, ask and I'll refund it

No form to fill in. No explanation needed.

The short version

Email hello@brookesai.com within 30 days of buying, tell me the email address you paid with, and I'll refund you in full. I won't ask you to justify it and I won't try to talk you out of it.

Why it's this simple

The whole point of putting a free course and a complete sample lesson on this site is so you can tell whether my way of explaining things suits you before you pay anything. If you've done that and it still turns out not to be for you, that's my judgement that was off, not yours. Keeping your £13.99 in those circumstances isn't something I'd feel good about.

What's covered

  • The Complete Beginner's Course — £13.99. 30-day refund, as above.
  • AI in 30 Minutes — free, so there's nothing to refund.

How to ask

  1. Email hello@brookesai.com.
  2. Include the email address you used at checkout, so I can find the order.
  3. That's it. You don't need to give a reason, though I'm always glad of one if you feel like sharing it — it's how the course gets better.

I read every email myself and usually reply within a day or two. Refunds are processed through Payhip back to your original payment method, and typically show up within five to ten working days depending on your bank.

Keeping the file

You'll have downloaded a PDF, and I have no way of taking it back — nor would I want to spend my life trying. I'd simply ask that if you've been refunded, you delete your copy and don't pass it on. That's an honesty-system request rather than a threat, and so far people have been decent about it.

Your rights beyond this policy

This policy sits on top of your legal rights, it doesn't replace them.

Under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 you normally have 14 days to cancel a purchase of digital content. Because the course is delivered immediately, Payhip's checkout asks you to consent to that immediate delivery — which, strictly speaking, waives the statutory 14-day right. My own 30-day policy above is deliberately more generous than the law requires, so in practice you're better off than the statutory position either way.

Separately, under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 digital content must be as described, of satisfactory quality and fit for purpose. If it isn't — if the file is corrupt, or it's plainly not what this website said it was — you're entitled to a repair, replacement or refund, and nothing on this page limits that. Those rights don't expire after 30 days.

If you're in the EU, your local consumer protections apply too, and Payhip acts as the seller of record for VAT purposes on those sales.

A note on this page

I've written this in plain English deliberately, and it reflects genuinely how I intend to behave. It isn't legal advice, and I'm not a solicitor. If something here matters a great deal to you, take your own advice — and do tell me if you think I've got something wrong.
