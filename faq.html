Getting started

I know nothing at all about this. Is that a problem?

It's the assumption. The free course starts from genuinely nothing — it walks you through installing the app, one tap at a time, including checking you've got the real one and not a copy. If you can send a text message, you're fine.

Do I need to be good with computers?

No. There's nothing to configure, no settings to get right, and nothing you can break. The whole app is one box that you type into.

Am I too late to this?

No — and this comes up more than anything else. There was no announcement and no lesson. It arrived in about eighteen months and everyone started talking about it as though it were obvious. You haven't missed anything; there just wasn't a class to miss.

Which app do I need — ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini?

Any of the three, and all of them are free to start. The courses use ChatGPT because it's the one most people have heard of, but everything works identically on the other two. Nothing is wasted if you switch later.

Will I have to pay for the AI app itself?

No. All three have proper free versions, and they're all you need for everything in these courses. There are paid tiers; you don't need one, possibly ever. If something is telling you that you must pay to try AI, treat it as a scam — that's covered in Lesson 4.

The free course

Is it actually free, or is it a trailer for the paid one?

Actually free, and actually complete. Four lessons, about thirty minutes. By the end you're set up on your phone and laptop, you've used it for something real, and you know the safety rules. Plenty of people will stop there and be perfectly well served — that's a good outcome, not a failure.

Do I have to give you my email?

No. The lessons are just there on the site — nothing is gated. You can send me your email if you'd like the printable cheat sheet, but you can also just read and print it here without telling me anything.

Do I need to do the lessons in order?

It's better if you do — Lesson 1 contains the one idea the rest depends on, and Lesson 2 gets you set up so that 3 and 4 make sense. But nothing stops you jumping about.

Will it stay free?

Yes.

Trust, safety and the honest bit

Who actually wrote these?

Joe Brookes — me. I'm an MSc Artificial Intelligence expert, which mostly means I spent a long time learning how this technology genuinely works before trying to explain it to anyone. More here.

Is AI safe to use?

Safe enough, if you know three things — and Lesson 4 of the free course covers all three. In short: don't type anything you wouldn't write on a postcard; check anything that matters, because it can be confidently wrong; and don't rely on it to judge your own ideas, because it agrees with you too readily.

Does it store what I type?

Yes — anything you type goes to the company's computers and is attached to your account. Depending on your settings it may be used to improve the system, and in some circumstances a person could read it. That's why the postcard rule exists. Module 2 of the full course goes through this properly.

Will these courses make me an AI expert?

No, and I wouldn't claim it. They'll make you competent and confident with a tool that will save you a few hours a week and take some awkward things off your list. That's a smaller promise than you'll see elsewhere, and it has the advantage of being true.

What do you do with my email address?
