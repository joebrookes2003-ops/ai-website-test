The idea the whole thing rests on

It is not a search engine

You've spent twenty-five years learning to type as few words as possible. Train times Manchester. Chicken recipe. Keywords, fragments — because that's what worked.

This is the opposite. It's a conversation, and it gets better the more you tell it, not less.

The old habit
You type

chicken recipe

You get a chicken recipe. Nothing you couldn't have found yourself.

The shift
You type

I've got two chicken breasts, half a bag of spinach and some rice that needs using up. There's two of us, I'm not a confident cook, and I've got about half an hour. What can I make?

Now you get something that's actually for you. And you can follow it with "I don't much like spinach" — because it remembers.

The questions people actually ask

Do I need to be good with computers?

No. If you can install an app and type a message, you can do this. Lesson 2 walks you through the setup one tap at a time, including the bit where it emails you to check it's really you — which is where most people wander off and don't come back.

Is the free course actually free, or is it a trailer for the paid one?

It's actually free, and it's actually complete. By the end you'll be set up, you'll have used it properly, and you'll know the safety rules. You can stop there and be perfectly well served. The full course goes further; it isn't the same thing withheld.

Do I have to give you my email?

Not to read the free course — it's just there on the site. If you want the printable cheat sheet sent over, you can send it to me, but that's your choice and nothing is gated behind it.
