The problem

Most businesses don't have an AI problem. They have a process problem.

Buying an AI tool and hoping it helps is how most automation projects fail. The work that's actually costing you money is usually hidden in the gaps between systems, in inboxes, and in the manual steps nobody has written down.

That's why we start by mapping what your people really do — not what the org chart says they do.

  • 01 Copy-paste between systems. Someone re-keys the same data into a CRM, a spreadsheet and an accounting package because nothing talks to anything else.
  • 02 Inboxes as a queue. Enquiries, invoices and approvals arrive by email and get triaged by hand, all day, by someone expensive.
  • 03 Documents read by humans. Contracts, purchase orders, timesheets and forms get opened, skimmed and typed up one at a time.
  • 04 Knowledge in one person's head. Work stops when a particular colleague is on holiday, because only they know the process.
  • 05 Reporting that eats a day a month. Numbers pulled from four places, pasted into a template, reconciled by hand.
  • 06 An AI pilot that went nowhere. A tool was bought, nobody adopted it, and the underlying process never changed.

What we automate

Three areas where AI workflows reliably pay for themselves

We don't sell a product. We build the workflow your process needs, using the right mix of AI models, integrations and plain automation — whatever actually solves it.

Document & data processing

Anything that arrives as a PDF, a scan, a form or an email attachment and currently gets read and typed up by a person.

  • Invoice and purchase-order extraction into your finance system
  • Contract and policy review with the key terms pulled out
  • Application and onboarding forms validated and filed automatically
  • Monthly reports assembled from multiple sources

Customer communication

The front door of your business — enquiries, questions and follow-ups — handled faster without losing the human touch where it matters.

  • Inbox triage: routed, prioritised and summarised before anyone reads it
  • Draft responses prepared from your own knowledge base, sent by a human
  • Lead qualification and CRM records updated automatically
  • Support deflection for the questions you answer fifty times a week

Internal operations

The connective tissue between departments and systems — the handoffs, approvals and status chasing that quietly consume the working week.

  • Approval chains that route themselves and chase their own deadlines
  • Employee and client onboarding run end to end
  • Data kept in sync across CRM, finance and operational tools
  • Internal assistants that answer staff questions from your own documents

How it works

Review first. Build second. Nothing speculative.

Every engagement follows the same four stages. You can stop after any of them, and you'll own everything produced up to that point.

  1. 01

    Process audit Free · 1 week

    A structured conversation with the people who do the work, plus a look at the systems involved. We map your current processes end to end and identify where time and money are actually going.

    You get A written map of your processes and a shortlist of automation candidates, ranked by return — yours to keep whether or not we work together.

  2. 02

    Scoped proposal Fixed price

    We pick one process — usually the highest-value, lowest-risk one — and write it up properly: what gets automated, what stays human, which systems are touched, what it costs to build and to run, and how we'll know it worked.

  3. 03

  4. 04

    Handover or ongoing support Your choice

    Documentation, access and ownership transfer to you. If you'd rather we kept monitoring and improving it, there's a support arrangement for that — but it's optional, and you're never locked in.

    You get Full documentation and ownership. No proprietary lock-in, no hostage systems.

Security & data

Your data stays yours

Most objections to AI in business are really objections about data handling. Reasonably so. Here's how we work.

  • Never used to train models

    We use enterprise API tiers where inputs and outputs are excluded from model training by contract. Your documents don't become someone else's training data.

  • Minimum necessary access

    We ask for the narrowest permissions that make the workflow function, scoped to the systems involved, and we hand credentials back at the end of the engagement.

  • UK GDPR from the start

    A data processing agreement is in place before we touch anything. We'll tell you plainly which sub-processors are involved and where data is handled.

  • Humans stay in the loop where it counts

    Anything with legal, financial or reputational consequence keeps a human approval step. Automation should remove the typing, not the judgement.
