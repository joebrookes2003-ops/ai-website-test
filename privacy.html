Privacy

What I do with your information

Short version: as little as possible, and nothing you wouldn't expect.

The short version

This site has no tracking, no analytics and no advertising cookies. I only ever hold your email address, and only if you've given it to me. I don't sell it, share it or add you to anything you didn't ask for.

Who's responsible

Brookes AI is run by Benjamin Brookes, a sole trader based in the United Kingdom. For UK GDPR purposes I'm the data controller. You can reach me at hello@brookesai.com about anything on this page.

Reading this website

You can read every page, including all four free lessons and the cheat sheet, without telling me anything at all. There's no signup wall, no analytics script, and no third-party tracker on any page.

Two things are loaded from other companies' servers so the site looks right: fonts from Google Fonts and icons from unpkg. Loading a file from a server necessarily reveals your IP address to that server. I don't receive that information and can't see it, but it's honest to say it happens.

Your site is served by Cloudflare Pages, which keeps basic server logs for security and abuse prevention. Again, that's Cloudflare's, not mine.

Cookies

I set no cookies. The site does use your browser's local storage for two small conveniences: remembering that you closed the announcement bar, and remembering which free lessons you've opened so the list can show your progress. That information never leaves your own device — I can't see it, and clearing your browser data removes it.

If you go through checkout, Payhip will set its own cookies. That's covered by their policy.

If you email me

The contact form on this site doesn't send anything anywhere by itself. It opens your own email app with the message ready, so you can see exactly what's being sent and you keep a copy. Nothing is transmitted until you press send.

When you do email me, I keep the message and your address for as long as it takes to help you, plus a reasonable period afterwards in case you follow up. Lawful basis: legitimate interests — you contacted me and expect a reply.

If you ask for the cheat sheet

I'll use your address to send you the cheat sheet, and to let you know when a new course is out if that's what you asked for. Nothing else. Every email I send has an unsubscribe, and asking me to remove you by reply works just as well. Lawful basis: consent, which you can withdraw whenever you like.

If you buy the course

Payment is handled entirely by Payhip. Your card details go to their payment processor and never touch this website or me — I never see a card number. Payhip passes me your email address and what you bought, so that I can deliver the course and handle any refund. They also collect and remit UK and EU VAT on the sale.

I keep purchase records for six years, because HMRC requires it for tax purposes. Lawful basis: legal obligation.

Payhip's own privacy policy governs what they do with your data, and is worth a read if you're interested: payhip.com/privacy.

Who else sees your information

Nobody I've not named above. Specifically: I don't sell data, I don't share it with advertisers, and I don't run any ad retargeting. There's no mailing-list company holding a copy of your address for me.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask me to show you what I hold about you, correct it, delete it, or stop using it. Email hello@brookesai.com and I'll deal with it within a month — realistically within a couple of days, because there won't be much to look through.

If you think I've handled your information badly and I haven't put it right, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Children

These courses are written for adults. I don't knowingly collect information from anyone under 16.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, I'll update the date below. I won't quietly broaden what I do with your data and hope nobody notices.

A note on this page

Written in plain English on purpose, and it reflects what actually happens. It isn't legal advice and I'm not a solicitor — if you're relying on it for something important, take your own advice.
